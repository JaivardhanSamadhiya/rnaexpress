## 1. Project goal

Develop and rigorously evaluate methods for selecting small RNA sequence changes
that alter localization. Under the current constraints, use free public measured
data and local computation. The immediate candidate is selecting two-base swaps
that preserve nucleotide composition in a six-mer nuclear-localization assay.

The raw swap check includes 592 held-out parent six-mers in 60 composition
classes after removing 17 low-count neighborhoods. Candidates and predictions
were fixed before examining raw scores. Parent/candidate neighborhoods overlap;
intervals resample composition classes, not individual reads or independent
biological experiments. Directional benefit is positive separately for increasing
and decreasing nuclear retention. Average directed log2-score changes are about
0.12 for the pair model; these are modest assay effects, not large therapeutic
changes. The published aggregate training labels already used these experiments.

The documented forward sequence orientation matches the raw data much better
than the reverse-complement mapping. The public counting script's zero-NES bug
does not establish that the published table is erroneous; its actual table-
