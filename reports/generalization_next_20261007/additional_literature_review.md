# Additional primary-source checks, 7 October 2026

[Taliaferro et al.,2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5107313/) experimentally varied sequence context around RBP motifs and found accessibility changes associated with binding and regulation. This supports testing structure-weighted motif effects. It also establishes that this biological idea itself is prior art; the present claim would need to concern successful excluded-context candidate-edit decisions under its own controls.

[GRASP,2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13280954/) already combines RNA structural graphs and sequence descriptors for multi-label localization classification. Its reported evaluation concerns intact-RNA annotations under cross-validation, rather than held-assay small-edit selection. The existence of structure-informed localization models rules out a blanket novelty claim for adding RNA structure.

[mRNABench](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12265608/) evaluates mature-RNA representations and explicitly examines homology effects on generalization. Its localization tasks are transcript-level measurements. This supports strict biological/allele exclusion but does not supply a substitute edit outcome. Mature-RNA pretraining is a relevant prior; larger parameter count is not sufficient justification for a resource-heavy download.

The new [coRBP map](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42014727/) describes compartmentalized known/putative RBP distributions and complexes. It is a possible independent mechanism annotation resource, not a candidate-edit validation experiment. Neural contexts and culture differences would still require separate certification before using it as a matched trans-context feature. No new localization outcome was downloaded during this check.
